39 Unary symbol; categorically determined sign EU conformity marks, status markers, legal labels, identifiers The best EU example is the CE marking: a single sign on a product means that the product has been assessed under the relevant EU rules and is intended to meet certain requirements. Formally, this is almost a unary predicate: CE(x) — this product is in the EU conformity state. Further examples would be EU labels, DGA logos for trusted data intermediaries, or status markers such as “gatekeeper” under the Digital Markets Act. Source: CE marking
40 Manifold / variety; several social classes as one thing European social, market, and legal variety Your intuition that “variety” may sound better is strong here. The EU is not a homogeneous mass but a structured plurality: citizens, workers, companies, consumers, regions, Member States, third-country nationals, institutions, markets, currency areas. The single market connects these classes through the four freedoms — goods, services, persons, and capital — while cohesion policy and social rights address inequalities between regions and groups. Better Regulation also works explicitly in cycles through preparation, impact assessment, implementation, and evaluation. Source: European Semester

The instances are national transpositions, administrative practices, and concrete administrative acts. Implementing acts and comitology are especially fitting: the Commission receives powers to set uniform conditions for implementing EU law. Source: Implementing acts

The primacy of EU law works like a kernel rule when EU law conflicts with national law. The abstract data structures here are: Member State, Union citizen, institution, competence, legal act, right, duty, procedure, sanction. Source: Founding agreements

EU examples: subsidiarity review, proportionality, fundamental-rights review, gender mainstreaming, SME test, risk assessment, language version. A traversal would be the application of one aspect across many legal acts and programmes. The EU has 24 official languages; legal language alignment alone is a real lenses/alignment problem. 48 Artificial neural networks and graph theory; networks, order in the multiverse The EU as a graph of institutions, states, markets, agencies, data flows, and platform networks This is about network structure. Edges: legal acts, money flows, data flows, lawsuits, competences, standards, trade relations. Artificial neural networks are the learning-capable special form of such networks; the EU increasingly regulates this level through AI, platform, and digital-market rules. The Digital Services Act regulates online services, the Digital Markets Act regulates gatekeeper platforms, and the AI Act sets the framework for AI systems. Source: Digital Services Act

Most important correction within your mapping

37 and 38 should remain separate.
37 is data and model management.
38 is the optimization of that management and of legal/policy processes.

40 should be called “variety” rather than “manifold” if you mean social classes.
“Manifold” fits better if you mean local charts, transitions, and overlaps. “Variety” fits better if you mean several structured classes as one composite thing.

51 is right as a candidate, but not as pure Bayesian statistics.
The EU is not a Bayesian machine. It is more of a deliberative evidence machine: statistics, expert opinions, interests, risks, legal principles, and political power are condensed into a decidable position.

55 is very strong.
If 50 is machine learning as a learning process, then 55 is AI as generator: a system produces outputs that causally intervene in society, administration, economy, and law.
