# EU Mapping of Mathematical Types 28–36

Yes — **subsidiarity belongs strongly to 34**, not primarily to 30.  
30 is more the **universal condition of validity**: _May the EU act at all?_  
34 is the **meta-probabilistic decision zone**: _Is EU-level action probably better, more effective, less fragmenting, and proportionate compared with national/regional/local action?_

I would map your list onto the EU as follows:

| **28** | Topology / combinatorics / discrete mathematics                  | **The EU’s space of competences and levels**                                  | The EU is not a simple central state, but a space of overlapping levels: EU, Member States, regions, municipalities, internal market, euro area, Schengen, foreign policy, budgetary order, and so on. That is more topological-combinatorial than hierarchical-linear. Important: your “28” here is a type number, not the current number of EU states; the EU currently has 27 Member States. Article 288 TFEU names the main EU legal acts: regulations, directives, decisions, recommendations, and opinions. [Source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=legissum%3Alegal_acts)                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **30** | Universal properties / universal connection                      | **Principle of conferral; treaty basis; primary law**                         | This is the highest validity structure: the EU may not simply act because something is politically desirable. It needs a competence from the treaties. EUR-Lex puts it clearly: conferral limits EU competences; subsidiarity and proportionality then govern the exercise of those competences. [Source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/glossary/principle-of-conferral.html)                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

An EU directive sets an objective, but the Member States choose the form and methods of implementation. That creates local legal sections that do not automatically glue together perfectly. Only with strong harmonisation or directly applicable regulations does the structure become more sheaf-like. Directives are binding as to the result, but leave national form and methods open; regulations are general, binding, and directly applicable. [Source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=legissum%3Alegal_acts) |
| **32** | Category                                                         | **The EU legal order as an overall system**                                   | The category is the whole system: objects are institutions, Member States, competences, legal acts, policy fields, courts, citizens, companies. Morphisms are the procedures and legal effects from 29. Here the EU is not merely an organisation, but a legal order with its own objects, arrows, compositions, and conditions of validity.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **33** | Functor / diagram / automation / applicative                     | **Translation machines between the EU level and the national level**          | Functors are the structure-preserving translations: Commission proposal → Parliament/Council → legal act → national transposition → administrative practice → judicial review. The ordinary legislative procedure fits especially well: the Commission proposes; Parliament and Council examine, amend, and adopt jointly. [Source](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/council-eu/decision-making/ordinary-legislative-procedure/)                                                                                                                                                            |
| **34** | Probability domain / meta-probability                            | **Subsidiarity, proportionality, impact assessment, risk assessment**         | This is the right place for subsidiarity. Subsidiarity does not only ask logically, “Does the EU have competence?” It asks evaluatively and prognostically, “Is EU action better than action at national, regional, or local level?” The protocol on subsidiarity and proportionality requires attention to regional/local dimensions; the Commission also works with impact assessments, problem analysis, and risk analysis. [Source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A12008E%2FPRO%2F02)                                                                        |
| **35** | Natural transformation / 1-transform / single database operation | **Amendment, interpretation, judicial clarification, legal-act operation**    | This is a coherent operation between two system states. Especially fitting: preliminary rulings by the Court of Justice of the EU. A national court asks a question, the Court gives a binding interpretation, and that interpretation works coherently for comparable cases in all Member States. This is almost a natural transformation between “previous national interpretation” and “harmonised EU interpretation.” [Source](https://curia.europa.eu/site/jcms/d2_5093/en/the-court-of-justice)                                                                                         |
| **36** | Database / SQL                                                   | **EUR-Lex, Official Journal, registers, CELEX, consolidated texts, case law** | This is the storage and query layer. EUR-Lex contains treaties, legal acts, consolidated texts, case law, national transposition measures, and legislative procedures; the Official Journal is the official publication for EU legal acts and other official information. [Source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/homepage.html?locale=en)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

## The cleanest short ordering

**28 = Space**  
The EU as a topological space of competences and levels.

**29 = Arrows**  
Legal acts and procedures as morphisms.

**30 = Condition of validity**  
Conferral: EU action needs a treaty basis.

**31 = Local data**  
Directives, national transposition, local legal sections: more presheaves than sheaves.

**32 = System category**  
The EU legal order as a category of objects and morphisms.

**33 = Translation**  
Functors: EU law is translated into national administration, courts, and implementation.

**34 = Decision under uncertainty**  
Subsidiarity, proportionality, impact assessment, risk.

**35 = Coherent operation**  
Interpretation, amendment, CJEU decision, update of a legal state.

**36 = Storage**  
EUR-Lex, Official Journal, registers, case-law database.

## Why subsidiarity fits exactly with 34

Subsidiarity is not merely an abstract universal principle. It is a **comparison under incomplete information**:

> Which level solves the problem better: EU, Member State, region, municipality?

That is not a pure yes/no question of competence. It is an assessment of effectiveness, fragmentation risk, closeness to citizens, implementation costs, internal-market effects, and side effects. That is exactly why subsidiarity belongs in your **meta-probability region 34**.

The sharp distinction would be:

**30:** May the EU act?  
**34:** Should the EU act here, rather than leaving the matter at a lower level?  
**35:** Which concrete operation changes the legal state?  
**36:** Where is this state stored, findable, and versioned?

Your placement “subsidiarity fits with 34” is therefore not only plausible, but structurally very strong.
