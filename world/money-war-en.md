# Money or War: Which Is the Greater Threat to the Planet?

**Money is probably the greater structural threat. War is the greater acute threat.**

The clean way to put it would be:

> **War can devastate the planet quickly; money can devastate it slowly and rationally.**

By “planet,” I do not mean the rocky sphere itself, but the biosphere, civilization, biodiversity, climate, food systems, and the conditions for human survival.

**War** is extremely dangerous because it destroys directly: people, cities, soils, energy infrastructure, trust, and institutions. Nuclear war in particular is a real civilizational threat. A *Nature Food* study modeled that soot in the atmosphere after nuclear detonations could trigger massive global food crises; in a large US-Russia scenario, it estimates more than five billion famine deaths. ([Nature Food](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0)) Conventional militarization is ecologically relevant too: SIPRI put global military expenditure in 2025 at US$2.887 trillion, with spending continuing to rise. ([SIPRI](https://www.sipri.org/media/press-release/2026/global-military-spending-rise-continues-european-and-asian-expenditures-surge)) A CEOBS/Scientists for Global Responsibility estimate puts military forces and military supply chains at around 5.5% of global greenhouse gas emissions, with weak data quality. ([CEOBS/SGR](https://ceobs.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/SGRCEOBS-Estimating_Global_MIlitary_GHG_Emissions_Nov22_rev.pdf))

But **money** — more precisely, the logic of return on capital, growth, debt, property, and the purchasability of politics — is embedded more deeply. War is a catastrophe mode. Money is the operating system. It decides what gets built, what does not get built, which risks are externalized, which forms of life have no price, which politicians get elected, which technologies scale, and which destruction appears as “growth.” That is exactly why climate destruction is so hard to stop: it is not irrational in the narrow economic sense; it is often profitable as long as the damage is externalized. The IPCC describes climate change as a threat to human well-being and planetary health and warns of a rapidly closing window for securing a livable future. ([IPCC](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/resources/spm-headline-statements/)) The IMF put explicit fossil-fuel subsidies in 2024 at US$0.73 trillion and implicit subsidies — environmental, health, and other costs that are not priced in — at US$6.7 trillion. ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/topics/climate-change/energy-subsidies)) That is almost the perfect illustration: destruction is not merely tolerated; it is organized through false prices.

So the difference is this:

| Dimension | War | Money |
|---|---:|---:|
| Speed | very high | slow to medium |
| Visibility | extremely visible | often invisible |
| Maximum damage | nuclear-catastrophic | ecological-civilizational catastrophic |
| Everyday presence | episodic | permanent |
| Moral camouflage | weak | strong: “market,” “growth,” “jobs,” “efficiency” |
| System depth | symptom and instrument | incentive architecture |

My hard answer: **The danger is not money as a medium of exchange, but money as the supreme judge of reality.** Once everything is reduced to a price, things without a market price lose: forests, species, stable climate systems, children in the future, truth, and social cohesion. War kills openly. Money often kills through accounting.

More precisely:

**War is the moment when civilization loses its inhibitions. Money is the mechanism that can systematically dismantle those inhibitions beforehand.**

That is why money is more dangerous in the long run: it operates even in peacetime. It can finance war, prolong fossil extraction, corrupt politics, distort media, redirect research, drive resources into status consumption instead of resilience, and discount the future. War is a fire. Money is the power grid that decides where sparks keep appearing.

But there is an important qualification: **money can also save.** Capital can scale solar energy, develop vaccines, protect forests, build low-carbon infrastructure, reduce poverty, and enable cooperation. War has far less positive reversibility. So money is not intrinsically evil. It is dangerous because it is an extremely powerful coordination instrument that, without ethical, ecological, and political limits, becomes a machine for optimized short-sightedness.

My ranking would be:

1. **Bad monetary system / wrong incentive order** — the greatest chronic threat.
2. **Nuclear war** — the greatest acute peak threat.
3. **Conventional war and militarization** — a massive amplifier of ecological, political, and social instability.

If one has to choose only one: **money is more dangerous than war, because war often emerges from failed order — and money is now a central part of that order.**
