How does money made of value, goodness level, and popularity level circulate when there are two angles?

Money no longer circulates as a simple stream from buyer to seller. It circulates as a double stream:

Goods/services flow from the seller to the buyer \text{Goods/services flow from the seller to the buyer}

Money flows from the buyer to the seller \text{Money flows from the buyer to the seller}

Both streams have a direction in angle space.

The central point is:

Every trade is a double angle exchange. \boxed{ \text{Every trade is a double angle exchange.} }

The buyer does not merely buy a product.
At the same time, the seller buys the buyer’s money.

1. The money object

A unit of money does not consist only of value:

M=m M = m

but of:

M=(m,g,b,ρ,h) M = (m,g,b,\rho,h)

where:

m=numerical value m = \text{numerical value}

g=degree of goodness g = \text{degree of goodness}

b=degree of popularity b = \text{degree of popularity}

ρ=confidence / certainty of the evaluation \rho = \text{confidence / certainty of the evaluation}

h=history / origin h = \text{history / origin}

The angle results from goodness and popularity:

θ=atan2⁡(b,g) \theta = \operatorname{atan2}(b,g)

So:

g=x-axis: good vs. evil g = \text{x-axis: good vs. evil}

b=y-axis: popular vs. unpopular b = \text{y-axis: popular vs. unpopular}

The amount of money is:

m m

The direction is:

θ \theta

The credibility of the direction is:

ρ \rho

A payment is therefore not merely:

100 100

but for example:

100∠35∘,ρ=0.82 100 \angle 35^\circ,\quad \rho=0.82

That means: 100 value units, with a specific goodness/popularity direction and a specific certainty.

2. Every actor has two angles

Every actor ii has:

θiK=buy angle \theta_i^K = \text{buy angle}

θiV=sell angle \theta_i^V = \text{sell angle}

The best definition is:

θiK=the direction I want to receive \boxed{ \theta_i^K = \text{the direction I want to receive} }

θiV=the direction I want to spend or sell \boxed{ \theta_i^V = \text{the direction I want to spend or sell} }

So:

  • The buy angle is an input filter.
  • The sell angle is an output signal.

An actor therefore becomes an angle transformer.

It receives things from one direction and emits things in another direction.

θiK→θiV \theta_i^K \rightarrow \theta_i^V

If both angles are almost equal, the actor is coherent.

If they are far apart, the actor earns from, or lives from, angle transformation.

3. Every trade has two angle checks

Assume:

  • Buyer AA buys from seller BB.
  • Seller BB delivers a product or service.
  • Buyer AA pays money.

Then there are two flows:

Product / service:
B ─────────────────────────▶ A

Money:
A ─────────────────────────▶ B

But now with angles:

Product flow:
B sells with θ_B^V ─────▶ A receives with θ_A^K

Money flow:
A pays with θ_A^V ──────▶ B receives with θ_B^K

So two angle distances must fit:

dX=d(θAK,θBV) d_X = d(\theta_A^K,\theta_B^V)

for the product stream.

And:

dM=d(θBK,θAV) d_M = d(\theta_B^K,\theta_A^V)

for the money stream.

This is extremely important.

The buyer asks:

Does the product I receive fit my buy angle?

The seller asks:

Does the money I receive fit my buy angle?

Because for the seller, money is the received good.

4. Circular distance decides tradability

The distance between two angles is:

d(α,β)=arccos⁡(cos⁡(α−β)) d(\alpha,\beta)=\arccos(\cos(\alpha-\beta))

So:

d∈[0,π] d \in [0,\pi]

Thus:

d=0 d=0

means perfect compatibility.

d=π d=\pi

means maximum opposition.

A simple compatibility function would be:

ϕ(d)=cos⁡(d2) \phi(d)=\cos\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)

Then:

d=0⇒ϕ=1 d=0 \Rightarrow \phi=1

d=π⇒ϕ=0 d=\pi \Rightarrow \phi=0

Trade becomes stronger the better the angles fit.

For a complete trade, both sides are needed:

Φ=ϕ(dX)⋅ϕ(dM)⋅ρX⋅ρM \Phi = \phi(d_X)\cdot \phi(d_M)\cdot \rho_X \cdot \rho_M

where:

dX=product angle distance d_X = \text{product angle distance}

dM=money angle distance d_M = \text{money angle distance}

ρX=confidence of the product angle \rho_X = \text{confidence of the product angle}

ρM=confidence of the money angle \rho_M = \text{confidence of the money angle}

If Φ\Phi is high, money circulates easily.

If Φ\Phi is low, discounts, fees, insurance, audits, rotation, or no trade arise.

5. The trade as a process

A complete trade looks like this:

Step 1: The buyer creates demand

Buyer AA says:

I want a good with direction near θAK \text{I want a good with direction near } \theta_A^K

For example:

θAK=30∘ \theta_A^K = 30^\circ

This means: the buyer seeks something that is good and reasonably popular.

Step 2: The seller offers

Seller BB says:

I sell a good with direction θBV \text{I sell a good with direction } \theta_B^V

For example:

θBV=55∘ \theta_B^V = 55^\circ

Then the product distance is:

dX=25∘ d_X = 25^\circ

This is relatively close. The buyer will likely accept.

Step 3: The buyer offers payment

Buyer AA does not pay with neutral money, but with money from his output angle:

θAV \theta_A^V

For example:

θAV=80∘ \theta_A^V = 80^\circ

The money may be very popular, but only moderately good.

Step 4: The seller checks payment

Seller BB has a buy angle for incoming values:

θBK \theta_B^K

For example:

θBK=70∘ \theta_B^K = 70^\circ

Then the money distance is:

dM=10∘ d_M = 10^\circ

The money fits the seller well.

Step 5: The trade is executed

If both distances are small enough:

dX≤εX d_X \leq \varepsilon_X

dM≤εM d_M \leq \varepsilon_M

then trade happens.

The buyer receives the product.

The seller receives the money.

But both receive not only value, but also angle quality.

6. What actually circulates?

Three things circulate at the same time:

Value \boxed{ \text{Value} }

Goodness \boxed{ \text{Goodness} }

Popularity \boxed{ \text{Popularity} }

But they do not circulate in the same way.

Value circulates through payment

A→B A \rightarrow B

The buyer loses numerical value.

The seller gains numerical value.

Goodness circulates through origin, production, and evaluation

Goodness arises or disappears through real actions:

  • clean supply chains
  • fair labor
  • low harm
  • good products
  • repair of damage
  • legality
  • environmental effect
  • social effect
  • transparency
  • audits
  • courts
  • government evaluation

Goodness is therefore not merely opinion. It is more strongly bound to real consequences and institutional review.

Popularity circulates through acceptance and attention

Popularity arises through:

  • demand
  • customer satisfaction
  • media
  • trends
  • advertising
  • social networks
  • cultural symbolism
  • political camps
  • boycotts
  • protests
  • influencers
  • herd behavior

Popularity moves faster than goodness.

Therefore popularity can rise faster and crash faster.

7. The balance-sheet change for the buyer

Before the purchase, buyer AA has:

MA=(mA,gA,bA) M_A = (m_A,g_A,b_A)

He pays:

ΔM=(p,gpay,bpay) \Delta M = (p,g_{\text{pay}},b_{\text{pay}})

Then his money stock falls:

mA′=mA−p m_A' = m_A - p

His outgoing money direction is:

θAV \theta_A^V

At the same time he receives a product:

X=(u,gX,bX) X = (u,g_X,b_X)

with utility uu, goodness gXg_X, popularity bXb_X.

When the product is consumed, it does not simply become money. It changes:

  • utility
  • standard of living
  • reputation
  • future preferences
  • political opinion
  • demand
  • possibly the actor’s own productivity

For a household:

Product→utility / satisfaction / opinion \text{Product} \rightarrow \text{utility / satisfaction / opinion}

For a firm:

Product→input / supply chain / new production angle \text{Product} \rightarrow \text{input / supply chain / new production angle}

This is important: if a firm buys a bad input, that bad angle can later enter its own product.

8. The balance-sheet change for the seller

Seller BB gives up a product:

XB=(u,gX,bX) X_B = (u,g_X,b_X)

and receives money:

Min=(p,gM,bM) M_{\text{in}} = (p,g_M,b_M)

His money stock rises:

mB′=mB+p m_B' = m_B + p

But his money quality also changes:

gB′=gB+gM g_B' = g_B + g_M

bB′=bB+bM b_B' = b_B + b_M

His new cash angle becomes:

θBcash=atan2⁡(bB′,gB′) \theta_B^{\text{cash}}= \operatorname{atan2}(b_B',g_B')

If many customers pay with good and popular money, the firm’s cash is pulled in that direction.

If many customers pay with toxic money, the firm’s cash is burdened.

That means:

Not only products have origin. Customer money also has origin. \boxed{ \text{Not only products have origin. Customer money also has origin.} }

9. Vector wallet: money is held as bundles

In practice, an actor should not store its wallet only as a sum.

It has many money lots:

Mi={Mi1,Mi2,Mi3,...} M_i = \{M_{i1},M_{i2},M_{i3},...\}

Each lot has:

(m,g,b,ρ,h) (m,g,b,\rho,h)

For example:

Lot 1: 500 value, good angle, high confidence
Lot 2: 200 value, popular but questionable
Lot 3: 100 value, toxic, low confidence

When the actor pays, he chooses which money to spend.

This is called coin selection.

Possible strategies:

Best money first

The actor pays with good money to get better prices.

Worst money first

The actor tries to get rid of toxic money.

Matching money first

The actor searches for money lots whose angles fit the seller’s buy angle.

Camouflage mixing

The actor mixes bad money with good money to create an acceptable average angle.

This is exactly where angle laundering emerges.

10. The seller can reject payment

The seller does not accept every kind of money equally.

He checks:

dM=d(θBK,θAV) d_M = d(\theta_B^K,\theta_A^V)

If the money is too far from his buy angle, there are several possibilities:

Rejection

dM>ε⇒no trade d_M > \varepsilon \Rightarrow \text{no trade}

Discount

The seller accepts the money, but only with a value loss:

meff=m⋅ϕ(dM) m_{\text{eff}} = m \cdot \phi(d_M)

For example:

100∠160∘ 100 \angle 160^\circ

may count for him only as:

62 62

Surcharge

The buyer must pay more:

prequired=pϕ(dM) p_{\text{required}} = \frac{p}{\phi(d_M)}

Rotation

An angle trader or bank rotates the money for a fee:

θAV→θBK \theta_A^V \rightarrow \theta_B^K

with cost:

Crot=λmtan⁡2(dM2) C_{\text{rot}}=\lambda m \tan^2\left(\frac{d_M}{2}\right)

The greater the angle distance, the more expensive the rotation.

Audit

The origin is checked. If the bad angle is based on false information, confidence or direction can be corrected.

11. The buyer can reject the product

At the same time, the buyer checks:

dX=d(θAK,θBV) d_X = d(\theta_A^K,\theta_B^V)

If the product is too far from the desired buy angle:

No purchase

The product remains unsold.

Price discount

The seller must become cheaper.

Product improvement

The seller invests in real improvement:

Value→Goodness \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{Goodness}

Marketing

The seller invests in perception:

Value→Popularity \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{Popularity}

Certification

The seller makes the angle more credible:

ρ↑ \rho \uparrow

This means: the product angle determines sales ability.

The money angle determines payment ability.

12. The two angles create four roles in every transaction

In every transaction there are four relevant angles:

θAK=buyer receives product \theta_A^K = \text{buyer receives product}

θAV=buyer gives money \theta_A^V = \text{buyer gives money}

θBK=seller receives money \theta_B^K = \text{seller receives money}

θBV=seller gives product \theta_B^V = \text{seller gives product}

This creates two matchings:

θAK↔θBV \theta_A^K \leftrightarrow \theta_B^V

for the good.

θBK↔θAV \theta_B^K \leftrightarrow \theta_A^V

for the money.

This is the complete circulation mechanics.

                 Product angle
          θ_B^V ───────────────▶ θ_A^K
          Seller                  Buyer


                   Money angle
          θ_B^K ◀─────────────── θ_A^V
          Seller                  Buyer

A trade is stable when both links are short.

A trade is unstable when one link is short and the other is long.

Buyer AA:

θAK=40∘ \theta_A^K = 40^\circ

θAV=60∘ \theta_A^V = 60^\circ

Seller BB:

θBK=55∘ \theta_B^K = 55^\circ

dM=d(55∘,60∘)=5∘ d_M = d(55^\circ,60^\circ)=5^\circ

Both sides fit.

The product fits the buyer.

The money fits the seller.

The trade is highly liquid.

Φ≈1 \Phi \approx 1

Money circulates easily.

14. Example: the product fits, the money does not

Buyer AA:

θAK=40∘ \theta_A^K = 40^\circ

dM=d(50∘,150∘)=100∘ d_M = d(50^\circ,150^\circ)=100^\circ

Consequence:

  • Buyer wants to buy.
  • Seller wants to sell.
  • But the seller distrusts the money.
  • A surcharge, rotation, audit, or different money is needed.

This is like a payment problem despite existing purchasing power.

The buyer has value, but the wrong angle.

15. Example: the money fits, the product does not

Buyer AA:

17. Circulation as a transformation chain

Every actor receives incoming values close to its buy angle:

θK \theta^K

and emits outgoing values close to its sell angle:

θV \theta^V

Thus every actor is a kind of economic angle transformer:

Ti:(m,g,b)in→(m′,g′,b′)out T_i: (m,g,b)_{\text{in}} \rightarrow (m',g',b')_{\text{out}}

or:

θiK→θiV \theta_i^K \rightarrow \theta_i^V

Examples:

Firm

Buys inputs and labor:

θfirmK \theta^K_{\text{firm}}

produces goods:

θfirmV \theta^V_{\text{firm}}

If it creates real quality:

g↑ g \uparrow

If it does good advertising:

b↑ b \uparrow

If it outsources harm:

g↓ g \downarrow

If it remains popular despite harm:

b↑,g↓ b \uparrow,\quad g \downarrow

Household

Receives wages:

θhouseholdK \theta^K_{\text{household}}

spends consumption money:

θhouseholdV \theta^V_{\text{household}}

Its purchase decisions change the popularity of firms.

Bank

Receives deposits:

θbankK \theta^K_{\text{bank}}

issues loans:

θbankV \theta^V_{\text{bank}}

It creates new money with an angle depending on borrower, purpose, and risk.

Government

Receives taxes:

θstateK \theta^K_{\text{state}}

spends public money:

θstateV \theta^V_{\text{state}}

It can define goodness through laws and shift angles through spending.

Media

Receive attention and money:

θmediaK \theta^K_{\text{media}}

emit popularity or unpopularity:

θmediaV \theta^V_{\text{media}}

They mainly influence bb, i.e. popularity.

Courts / audits

Receive cases, evidence, and fees:

θcourt/auditK \theta^K_{\text{court/audit}}

emit confidence and goodness corrections:

θcourt/auditV \theta^V_{\text{court/audit}}

They mainly influence gg and ρ\rho.

18. The most important circulation formula

For a transaction between buyer AA and seller BB:

dX=d(θAK,θBV) d_X = d(\theta_A^K,\theta_B^V)

dM=d(θBK,θAV) d_M = d(\theta_B^K,\theta_A^V)

Then:

Trade volume=Q⋅ϕ(dX)⋅ϕ(dM)⋅ρX⋅ρM \text{Trade volume} = Q \cdot \phi(d_X)\cdot \phi(d_M)\cdot \rho_X\cdot\rho_M

Or compactly:

QT=Q0Φ Q_T = Q_0 \Phi

with:

Φ=ϕ(dX)ϕ(dM)ρXρM \Phi = \phi(d_X)\phi(d_M)\rho_X\rho_M

If Φ\Phi is high, much circulates.

If Φ\Phi is low, little circulates.

If many actors have incompatible buy and sell angles, the liquidity of the whole economy falls.

19. How value buys goodness

Value can buy goodness, but only through real change.

For example:

Value→better working conditions \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{better working conditions}

Value→clean energy \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{clean energy}

Value→compensation for damage \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{compensation for damage}

Value→product safety \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{product safety}

Value→supply-chain inspection \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{supply-chain inspection}

Then:

g↑ g \uparrow

A possible simulation rule:

Δg=ηg⋅log⁡(1+Ig)⋅Rreal⋅ρaudit \Delta g = \eta_g \cdot \log(1+I_g)\cdot R_{\text{real}}\cdot \rho_{\text{audit}}

where:

Ig=investment in real improvement I_g = \text{investment in real improvement}

Rreal=reality factor R_{\text{real}} = \text{reality factor}

ρaudit=audit confidence \rho_{\text{audit}} = \text{audit confidence}

If a firm only claims to be good but makes no real improvement:

Rreal≈0 R_{\text{real}} \approx 0

Then, at most, popularity rises briefly, but not real goodness.

20. How value buys popularity

Value can buy popularity more easily:

Value→advertising \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{advertising}

Value→discounts \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{discounts}

Value→influencers \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{influencers}

Value→media campaigns \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{media campaigns}

Value→sponsorship \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{sponsorship}

Then:

b↑ b \uparrow

A possible simulation rule:

Δb=ηb⋅log⁡(1+Ib)⋅Mmedia⋅Strend−Bbacklash \Delta b = \eta_b \cdot \log(1+I_b)\cdot M_{\text{media}}\cdot S_{\text{trend}} - B_{\text{backlash}}

where:

Ib=advertising / PR spending I_b = \text{advertising / PR spending}

Mmedia=media amplification M_{\text{media}} = \text{media amplification}

Strend=trend factor S_{\text{trend}} = \text{trend factor}

Bbacklash=backlash B_{\text{backlash}} = \text{backlash}

Popularity is faster than goodness, but less stable.

Therefore often:

b↑,g remains the same b \uparrow,\quad g \text{ remains the same}

or even:

b↑,g↓ b \uparrow,\quad g \downarrow

This is the case:

popular but evil \text{popular but evil}

21. How popularity can buy goodness

Popularity cannot buy goodness directly, but it can make reforms easier.

A popular firm has:

  • more customer trust
  • more political capital
  • easier access to employees
  • more patience from investors
  • higher pricing power
  • less resistance to change

Then it can transform popularity into real reform capacity:

b→g b \rightarrow g

For example:

Δg=ηbg⋅b⋅Ig⋅ρ \Delta g = \eta_{bg}\cdot b \cdot I_g \cdot \rho

But here too, real investment is required.

Popularity alone is not goodness.

It is only a resource with which goodness can be produced more easily.

22. How goodness can buy popularity

Goodness can generate popularity, but only if it becomes visible.

g→b g \rightarrow b

This requires:

  • transparency
  • free media
  • credible audits
  • understandable communication
  • education
  • time
  • trust

A possible rule:

Δb=ηgb⋅g⋅Tvisibility⋅ρ \Delta b = \eta_{gb}\cdot g \cdot T_{\text{visibility}}\cdot \rho

where:

Tvisibility=visibility T_{\text{visibility}} = \text{visibility}

If nobody sees the goodness, popularity barely rises.

Therefore a good but invisible firm can remain unpopular.

23. The dangerous circulation: angle laundering

Angle laundering arises when someone turns bad value into a better apparent angle.

For example:

1000∠160∘→900∠40∘ 1000 \angle 160^\circ \rightarrow 900 \angle 40^\circ

but without real improvement.

This can happen through:

  • subsidiaries
  • intermediaries
  • fake audits
  • PR campaigns
  • donations
  • bought ratings
  • artificial popularity
  • bot networks
  • tax and angle havens

Then apparently:

g↑,b↑ g \uparrow,\quad b \uparrow

but confidence should fall:

ρ↓ \rho \downarrow

and history hh should become suspicious.

Therefore the system needs an origin trail:

h=transaction history h = \text{transaction history}

Without history, angle money becomes washable.

24. Circulation in the labor market

The labor market is especially important.

A firm buys labor.

A worker sells labor.

For the firm, labor is an input:

θfirmK \theta_{\text{firm}}^K

For the worker, wages are an incoming value:

θworkerK \theta_{\text{worker}}^K

At the same time:

  • The worker sells labor power with his sell angle.
  • The firm pays wages with its sell angle.

So again there are two matchings:

dlabor=d(θfirmK,θworkerV) d_{\text{labor}} = d(\theta_{\text{firm}}^K,\theta_{\text{worker}}^V)

dwage=d(θworkerK,θfirmV) d_{\text{wage}} = d(\theta_{\text{worker}}^K,\theta_{\text{firm}}^V)

If the labor fits but the wage angle is bad, the worker demands more wage.

If the wage is good but the activity is bad, he can still refuse.

This explains real cases:

high wage, bad employer \text{high wage, bad employer}

low wage, good purpose \text{low wage, good purpose}

popular firm, bad working conditions \text{popular firm, bad working conditions}

unpopular job, high social value \text{unpopular job, high social value}

25. Circulation in the credit market

In credit, money is newly created or shifted across time.

A bank issues credit:

θbankV \theta_{\text{bank}}^V

The debtor receives credit:

θdebtorK \theta_{\text{debtor}}^K

Later the debtor repays:

θdebtorV \theta_{\text{debtor}}^V

The bank receives repayment:

θbankK \theta_{\text{bank}}^K

So:

dcredit=d(θdebtorK,θbankV) d_{\text{credit}}=d(\theta_{\text{debtor}}^K,\theta_{\text{bank}}^V)

drepayment=d(θbankK,θdebtorV) d_{\text{repayment}}=d(\theta_{\text{bank}}^K,\theta_{\text{debtor}}^V)

If the credit serves a good purpose:

gcredit↑ g_{\text{credit}} \uparrow

If the credit finances popular speculation:

bcredit↑,gcredit uncertain b_{\text{credit}} \uparrow,\quad g_{\text{credit}} \text{ uncertain}

If the credit finances toxic activities:

gcredit↓ g_{\text{credit}} \downarrow

Then interest rates or collateral requirements rise.

The interest rate then consists of:

i=i0+idefault+iangle risk+iρ i = i_0 + i_{\text{default}} + i_{\text{angle risk}} + i_{\rho}

So:

Interest=time price+risk+angle penalty \text{Interest} = \text{time price} + \text{risk} + \text{angle penalty}

26. Circulation in the state

The state receives taxes:

θstateK \theta_{\text{state}}^K

and spends public money:

θstateV \theta_{\text{state}}^V

Taxes from toxic sources can be problematic:

Should the state accept bad money? \text{Should the state accept bad money?}

If yes, it may need to cleanse it:

θtoxic→θpublicly legitimate \theta_{\text{toxic}} \rightarrow \theta_{\text{publicly legitimate}}

through:

  • courts
  • transparency
  • redistribution
  • repair of harm

27. Circulation between countries

Every country has its own axes.

What is good in country A is not necessarily good in country B.

So angle translation is needed:

TA→B(θ) T_{A\rightarrow B}(\theta)

An export transaction then has:

θexport, A→TA→B(θexport, A) \theta_{\text{export, A}} \rightarrow T_{A\rightarrow B}(\theta_{\text{export, A}})

Example:

40∘ in country A 40^\circ \text{ in country A}

29. What the two angles mean economically

The difference between buy angle and sell angle is the core of business logic:

si=d(θiK,θiV) s_i = d(\theta_i^K,\theta_i^V)

This is the actor’s angle spread.

Small spread

si≈0 s_i \approx 0

The actor buys and sells in a similar direction.

That means:

  • coherent
  • credible
  • little transformation work
  • little arbitrage
  • high trustworthiness

Large spread

si≫0 s_i \gg 0

The actor buys in one direction and sells in another.

That can be good or bad.

Good variant

It buys bad inputs and improves them for real.

θK=bad \theta^K = \text{bad}

θV=good \theta^V = \text{good}

Then it creates real value improvement.

Bad variant

It buys bad inputs and only sells them in better packaging.

θK=bad \theta^K = \text{bad}

θV=apparently good \theta^V = \text{apparently good}

Then it practices angle laundering.

Extractive variant

It buys good inputs and sells bad outputs.

θK=good \theta^K = \text{good}

θV=bad \theta^V = \text{bad}

Then it destroys goodness or trust.

31. Money circulation becomes selective

In normal money:

100=100 100 = 100

In your system:

100∠30∘≠100∠150∘ 100 \angle 30^\circ \neq 100 \angle 150^\circ

Therefore money no longer circulates equally everywhere.

Zones form:

Clean high-confidence circuit

Good money circulates among trustworthy actors.

g↑,b↑,ρ↑ g \uparrow,\quad b \uparrow,\quad \rho \uparrow

There, interest rates are low, trade is fast, and spreads are small.

Popular but questionable circuit

Popular money circulates quickly, but with risk.

b↑,g uncertain b \uparrow,\quad g \text{ uncertain}

There are bubbles and scandals.

Good but unpopular circuit

Good but unpopular activities require patience, subsidies, or education.

g↑,b↓ g \uparrow,\quad b \downarrow

There is a threat of underfunding.

Toxic circuit

Bad money circulates in shadow markets or with high discounts.

g↓,b↓,ρ↓ g \downarrow,\quad b \downarrow,\quad \rho \downarrow

There, interest rates are high, fraud is common, and liquidity is low.

32. Why circulation does not automatically become moral

A common mistake would be:

If goodness and popularity are embedded in money, the economy automatically becomes good.

No.

The system makes moral direction tradable.

That also makes it manipulable.

It leads to three possible forms:

Real improvement

Value→real goodness \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{real goodness}

Symbolic improvement

Value→popularity \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{popularity}

Fraudulent improvement

Value→apparent goodness \text{Value} \rightarrow \text{apparent goodness}

Therefore ρ\rho, confidence, is absolutely central.

Without confidence, the angle becomes a propaganda label.

With confidence, the angle becomes verifiable.

33. The shortest mechanism

The shortest complete description is:

Actors receive through their buy angle and send through their sell angle. \boxed{ \text{Actors receive through their buy angle and send through their sell angle.} }

Every trade checks:

Does the sell angle of the good fit the buyer’s buy angle? \boxed{ \text{Does the sell angle of the good fit the buyer's buy angle?} }

and:

Does the sell angle of the money fit the seller’s buy angle? \boxed{ \text{Does the sell angle of the money fit the seller's buy angle?} }

If yes, value, goodness, and popularity flow easily.

If no, the result is:

  • discounts
  • surcharges
  • rotation
  • audit costs
  • insurance
  • waiting time
  • shadow markets
  • reputation risks
  • political conflicts

Money circulation is therefore no longer a circle, but a directed network:

Value flows along compatible angles. \boxed{ \text{Value flows along compatible angles.} }

Goodness flows along real improvement and recognition. \boxed{ \text{Goodness flows along real improvement and recognition.} }

Popularity flows along attention and approval. \boxed{ \text{Popularity flows along attention and approval.} }

The two angles of every actor determine what it wants to absorb and what it emits into the world.

That is exactly where the economic dynamic comes from.
