# Are Meta-Systems and Properties Traded in This Economy?

## Question

> Of all your transactions, buying, selling, and markets: are meta-systems and properties what is being traded?

## Answer

Yes — **in this economic model, what is essentially traded are meta-systems and properties**, but more precisely:

> What is traded is **not naked meta-systems themselves**, but **positions, rights, claims, roles, and privileges inside meta-systems**.  
> And what is traded is **not merely properties**, but **recognized, system-effective properties** that generate access, rank, risk, obligation, and advantage.

That is the core.

A bicycle, a job, a security, or a plot of land is then not simply a thing with a price. It is a **carrier** for an entire tree of properties and meta-rules.

---

## 1. What is really the object of trade in this economy?

In a normal economy, one sees:

```text
commodity ↔ money
```

In your economy, one sees rather:

```text
status tree ↔ entitlement tree
```

Or:

```text
person with properties
+
object with access schema
+
market rule
→
new status-and-claim state
```

So the trade object is usually a package consisting of:

```text
physical carrier
+ usage right
+ status effect
+ voucher binding
+ privilege
+ obligation
+ risk
+ trust evaluation
+ career effect
```

Example: a work laptop is not simply a “laptop.”

It is:

```text
Laptop = {
  physical device,
  productivity right,
  purpose binding: work,
  return obligation,
  status requirement: S2 technical,
  trust requirement,
  possible career effect
}
```

The thing is only the visible body. The actual economic content is meta-systemic.

---

## 2. Difference between thing, property, and meta-system

Three levels have to be separated.

### Level 1: Thing / carrier

This is the concrete object:

```text
house
car
laptop
land parcel
job
insurance contract
share
service
```

These things exist materially or institutionally.

### Level 2: Properties

These are attributed characteristics:

```text
scarce
risky
necessary
luxurious
work-relevant
status-relevant
trustworthy
need-critical
ecologically sensitive
productivity-enhancing
```

These properties determine how the thing is treated.

### Level 3: Meta-system

This is the order that decides **which properties count and what consequences they have**:

```text
status order
voucher order
market rule
insurance system
career system
land law
asset classification
risk assessment
need hierarchy
```

The meta-system says, for example:

```text
If object = work-relevant
and person = S2 technical
and voucher = productivity
then access permitted.
```

So:

> The thing is traded, but only as the carrier of a meaning determined by a meta-system.

---

## 3. Are meta-systems themselves tradable?

Partly, but cautiously.

A meta-system itself is, for example:

```text
the status hierarchy
the market order
the voucher logic
the insurance model
the land allocation rule
the asset classification
```

These systems are normally **not directly sold** like goods. Otherwise someone could buy the rules themselves, and that would immediately become oligarchy.

What can be traded or acquired is:

```text
positions inside the meta-system
access rights
voting rights
trusteeship rights
curator rights
examination rights
license rights
priority rights
status levels
```

Example:

Not tradable in a clean sense:

```text
"I buy the rule according to which land is allocated."
```

Tradable or acquirable:

```text
"I acquire a land-use right through contribution."
"I receive governance votes on the land council."
"I reach S4 land-trustee status."
```

So:

> It is not the meta-system as a whole that is traded, but **rank, access, and agency inside the meta-system**.

That is an important protection against corruption.

---

## 4. Are properties tradable?

Here too: **partly, but not all directly**.

A property such as:

```text
trustworthy
competent
S3
need-critical
risk-capable
```

cannot simply be transferred like an apple.

Mara cannot say:

```text
I sell you my competence.
I sell you my trust score.
I sell you my S3 status.
```

That would be dangerous, because status would become capital again.

But properties can be:

```text
acquired,
recognized,
verified,
lost,
certified,
transferred with purpose binding,
represented as a voucher
```

Example:

```text
Mara has the property: S3 technical.
```

This property itself is not sellable.

But rights can arise from it:

```text
tool access
job quota
infrastructure asset access
housing priority near deployment site
technical governance vote
```

Some of these rights may be tradable or delegable. The property itself remains bound to Mara.

Very important:

> Properties are often **not the traded good**, but the **condition from which traded goods arise**.

---

## 5. What does “buying” mean then?

In this economy, buying no longer primarily means:

```text
I give number X and receive thing Y.
```

Rather:

```text
I satisfy the access schema of an object
and my claim tree is transformed.
```

So:

```text
buying = embedding a new right into one's own status tree
```

Example:

```text
Mara buys a work vehicle.
```

This actually means:

```text
Mara receives a purpose-bound usage right to a vehicle,
because her status, her vouchers, and her work property fit.
```

After the transaction, Mara does not simply have less “balance,” but a changed social state:

```text
before:
  productivity voucher available
  no vehicle right

after:
  productivity voucher consumed
  vehicle right active
  maintenance obligation active
  work-performance potential increased
```

Buying is therefore a **status rewrite**.

---

## 6. What does “selling” mean then?

Selling does not merely mean:

```text
I give away a thing and receive money.
```

Rather:

```text
I transfer, release, or transform a right
that is bound to conditions.
```

Example:

An asset curator sells an infrastructure share.

That is not merely:

```text
Viktor sells a stock.
```

Rather:

```text
Viktor gives an income right,
a voting right,
a risk obligation,
and curator responsibility
to another eligible person.
```

The buyer must be suitable.

```text
Not everyone may buy.
Not everyone may hold.
Not everyone may resell.
```

Selling therefore becomes the **controlled transfer of meta-systemic rights**.

## 7. What is a market?

In your model, a market is itself a meta-system.

A market is not merely a place where supply and demand form prices. A market is a **rule machine** that decides:

```text
Which properties count?
Which persons may participate?
Which objects are accessible?
Which vouchers apply?
Which status effects arise?
Which risks are transferred?
Which obligations remain?
```

So:

```text
market = meta-system for transforming claim trees
```

A market takes persons and objects as input and produces new entitlement states as output.

```text
Market(P, O) → P'
```

Or with two parties:

```text
Market(A, B, O) → A', B', O'
```

## 13. Hard distinction: property vs. right

A property is:

```text
Mara is competent.
Mara is S3.
Mara is trustworthy.
Mara is in need.
Mara is risk-capable.
```

A right is:

```text
Mara may use tool X.
Mara may buy asset Y.
Mara receives housing priority.
Mara may train people.
Mara may vote in the council.
```

The property generates the right, but it is not identical with the right.

This is decisive.

Because if properties become directly tradable, abuse emerges:

```text
Rich people buy status.
Status becomes property.
Privileges become inheritable.
The meta-system is corrupted.
```

Therefore the rule should be:

```text
Status: not directly sellable
Competence: not directly sellable
Trust: not directly sellable
Basic rights: never sellable
Vouchers: partly transferable
Usage rights: transferable under control
Assets: transferable with status binding
Governance rights: very strictly bound
```
